## Core Accounting Concepts

### Double-Entry Bookkeeping
Double-entry bookkeeping is the fundamental principle of accounting where every transaction affects at least two accounts. For every debit entry, there must be a corresponding credit entry, maintaining the accounting equation: **Assets = Liabilities + Equity**.

### Chart of Accounts
A chart of accounts is a list of all accounts available for recording transactions in the general ledger of a company. Accounts are typically organized into categories:
- **Assets**: Current assets, fixed assets, intangible assets
- **Liabilities**: Current liabilities, long-term liabilities
- **Equity**: Common stock, retained earnings, paid-in capital
- **Revenue**: Sales, service revenue, other income
- **Expenses**: Operating expenses, cost of goods sold, depreciation

### Accounting Equation
**Assets = Liabilities + Stockholders' Equity**

This fundamental equation ensures that a company's assets are financed either through borrowing (liabilities) or through ownership investment (equity).

### Accrual vs. Cash Accounting
- **Accrual Accounting**: Revenue and expenses are recorded when earned or incurred, not when cash is received or paid. This is the standard for GAAP and IFRS.
- **Cash Accounting**: Revenue and expenses are recorded only when cash changes hands. Simpler but less accurate for financial analysis.

### Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP)
GAAP is a set of accounting standards and principles established by the Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB) in the United States. Key principles include:
- Consistency: Apply the same accounting methods across periods
- Conservatism: Understate assets and revenues when uncertain
- Matching Principle: Match expenses to the revenues they generate
- Full Disclosure: Report all material information

## Financial Statements

### Income Statement (Profit & Loss)
The income statement shows a company's profitability over a specific period. Structure:

```
Revenue
- Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)
= Gross Profit
- Operating Expenses (SG&A, R&D, Depreciation)
= Operating Income (EBIT)
- Interest Expense
+ Other Income
= Income Before Taxes
- Income Tax Expense
= Net Income
```

**Key Metrics:**
- **Gross Margin**: (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue
- **Operating Margin**: Operating Income / Revenue
- **Net Margin**: Net Income / Revenue

### Balance Sheet
The balance sheet presents a snapshot of a company's financial position at a specific date. It shows what the company owns (assets), what it owes (liabilities), and what shareholders own (equity).

**Balance Sheet Structure:**
- **Assets**: Cash, receivables, inventory, PP&E, intangibles
- **Liabilities**: Accounts payable, debt, accrued expenses
- **Stockholders' Equity**: Common stock, retained earnings, treasury stock

### Cash Flow Statement
The cash flow statement tracks how cash moves in and out of a business across three activities:

1. **Operating Activities**: Cash from core business operations
   - Adjustments: Add back non-cash charges (depreciation, amortization)
   - Changes in working capital: receivables, payables, inventory

2. **Investing Activities**: Cash from buying/selling assets
   - Capital expenditures, acquisitions, sale of investments

3. **Financing Activities**: Cash from debt and equity transactions
   - Debt issuance/repayment, stock issuance/buyback, dividends

**Free Cash Flow (FCF)** = Operating Cash Flow - Capital Expenditures

### Statement of Changes in Equity
Shows how stockholders' equity changed during the period:
- Beginning equity
- + Net income
- + Stock issuance
- - Dividends paid
- - Share repurchases
- = Ending equity

## Financial Ratios & Analysis

### Liquidity Ratios
Measure a company's ability to pay short-term obligations.

- **Current Ratio** = Current Assets / Current Liabilities
  - Benchmark: 1.5 - 3.0
  - Higher indicates better short-term liquidity

- **Quick Ratio** = (Current Assets - Inventory) / Current Liabilities
  - More conservative than current ratio
  - Excludes less liquid inventory

- **Cash Ratio** = Cash & Equivalents / Current Liabilities
  - Most conservative liquidity measure

### Solvency Ratios
Assess a company's ability to meet long-term obligations.

- **Debt-to-Equity Ratio** = Total Debt / Total Equity
  - Higher indicates more financial leverage
  - Varies by industry

- **Debt-to-Assets Ratio** = Total Debt / Total Assets
  - Shows proportion of assets financed by debt

- **Interest Coverage Ratio** = EBIT / Interest Expense
  - Measures ability to service debt
  - Higher is better; below 2.0 is concerning

- **Debt Service Coverage Ratio** = Net Operating Income / Total Debt Service
  - For lending decisions

### Profitability Ratios
Evaluate how efficiently a company generates profit.

- **Return on Assets (ROA)** = Net Income / Average Total Assets
  - Shows profit generated per dollar of assets

- **Return on Equity (ROE)** = Net Income / Average Stockholders' Equity
  - Return generated for shareholders
  - DuPont Analysis: ROE = Net Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier

- **Return on Invested Capital (ROIC)** = NOPAT / Invested Capital
  - Measures efficiency of capital deployment

- **Gross Profit Margin** = Gross Profit / Revenue
- **Operating Profit Margin** = Operating Income / Revenue
- **Net Profit Margin** = Net Income / Revenue

### Efficiency Ratios
Measure how effectively a company uses its assets.

- **Asset Turnover Ratio** = Revenue / Average Total Assets
  - Higher indicates more efficient use of assets

- **Inventory Turnover** = COGS / Average Inventory
  - How quickly inventory is sold
  - Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO) = 365 / Inventory Turnover

- **Receivables Turnover** = Revenue / Average Accounts Receivable
  - Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) = 365 / Receivables Turnover

- **Payables Turnover** = COGS / Average Accounts Payable
  - Days Payable Outstanding (DPO) = 365 / Payables Turnover

### Valuation Ratios

- **Price-to-Earnings (P/E)** = Stock Price / Earnings Per Share
  - Compares stock price to profitability
  - Lower P/E may indicate undervaluation

- **Price-to-Book (P/B)** = Market Cap / Book Value of Equity
  - Compares market to accounting value

- **Price-to-Sales (P/S)** = Market Cap / Total Revenue
  - Less affected by earnings quality than P/E

- **Enterprise Value-to-EBITDA (EV/EBITDA)** = Enterprise Value / EBITDA
  - Key multiple for M&A and valuation

- **PEG Ratio** = P/E Ratio / Earnings Growth Rate
  - Adjusts P/E for growth expectations

## Investment Fundamentals

### Types of Securities

**Equity Securities:**
- Common Stock: Ownership interest with voting rights and residual claims
- Preferred Stock: Fixed dividend, priority over common stock in liquidation
- Warrants & Options: Derivatives giving rights to buy/sell at set price

**Debt Securities:**
- Bonds: Fixed-income instruments with coupon payments
- Notes: Shorter maturity bonds
- Debentures: Unsecured bonds backed by issuer's creditworthiness
- Convertible Bonds: Can be converted to equity

### Bond Fundamentals
- **Par Value (Face Value)**: Amount repaid at maturity
- **Coupon Rate**: Annual interest rate
- **Yield to Maturity (YTM)**: Total return if held to maturity
- **Duration**: Sensitivity to interest rate changes
- **Credit Rating**: Assessment of default risk (AAA to D scale)

Bond Price and Yield Relationship:
- When interest rates rise, bond prices fall
- When interest rates fall, bond prices rise
- Longer duration bonds are more sensitive to rate changes

### Stock Valuation
- **Book Value Per Share** = Stockholders' Equity / Shares Outstanding
- **Earnings Per Share (EPS)** = Net Income / Weighted Average Shares
- **Dividend Per Share** = Total Dividends / Shares Outstanding
- **Dividend Yield** = Annual Dividend Per Share / Stock Price

### Risk and Return
- **Expected Return** = Σ(Probability × Return) for all scenarios
- **Variance & Standard Deviation**: Measures volatility of returns
- **Beta**: Stock's volatility relative to market
  - Beta > 1: More volatile than market
  - Beta < 1: Less volatile than market
  - Beta = 1: Moves with market

### Modern Portfolio Theory
- Portfolio return is weighted average of individual returns
- Diversification reduces risk without proportionally reducing return
- Efficient frontier: Optimal portfolios offering maximum return for given risk
- Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM): Expected Return = Risk-Free Rate + Beta × (Market Return - Risk-Free Rate)

## Corporate Finance

### Capital Structure Decisions
The mix of debt and equity financing affects the company's:
- **Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)**: Average cost of all capital sources
  - WACC = (E/V × Re) + (D/V × Rd × (1-Tc))
  - Where: E=Equity value, D=Debt value, V=Total value, Re=Cost of equity, Rd=Cost of debt, Tc=Tax rate

- **Financial Leverage**: Using debt to amplify returns
- **Pecking Order Theory**: Prefer internal funds, then debt, then equity
- **Trade-off Theory**: Balance debt benefits (tax shield) against bankruptcy costs

### Dividends and Share Buybacks

**Dividend Policy:**
- Cash Dividend: Direct payment to shareholders
- Stock Dividend: Issuance of additional shares
- Dividend Payout Ratio = Total Dividends / Net Income
- Ex-Dividend Date: Last day to own stock to receive dividend

**Share Repurchases (Buybacks):**
- Company repurchases its own stock
- Reduces share count, potentially increasing EPS
- Returns capital to shareholders
- Alternative to dividends with potential tax benefits

### Mergers & Acquisitions

**Types of Deals:**
- **Horizontal Merger**: Competitors combine
- **Vertical Merger**: Companies in supply chain combine
- **Conglomerate Merger**: Unrelated companies combine

**Valuation in M&A:**
- Comparable Company Analysis: Compare to similar firms
- Precedent Transactions: Look at historical deals
- Discounted Cash Flow (DCF): Project future cash flows

**Deal Structure:**
- Stock Purchase vs. Asset Purchase
- All-cash, all-stock, or mixed consideration
- Earnout: Contingent payments based on future performance

### Capital Budgeting

Techniques for evaluating long-term investments:

- **Net Present Value (NPV)**: PV of future cash flows minus initial investment
  - Positive NPV adds value
  - Decision rule: Accept if NPV > 0

- **Internal Rate of Return (IRR)**: Discount rate making NPV = 0
  - Represents project's return
  - Limitation: Conflicts with NPV in some scenarios

- **Payback Period**: Time to recover initial investment
  - Simple but ignores time value of money

- **Profitability Index** = PV of Future Cash Flows / Initial Investment
  - Useful when capital is limited

## Risk Management

### Types of Risk

**Business Risk**: Variability in operating income due to:
- Industry cyclicality
- Competition
- Product obsolescence
- Operating leverage

**Financial Risk**: Additional volatility from financial leverage
- Increases with debt levels
- Magnifies equity returns and risk

**Systematic Risk**: Market-related risk affecting all securities
- Economic recessions
- Interest rate changes
- Inflation
- Cannot be diversified away

**Unsystematic Risk**: Company-specific risk
- Management changes
- Product recalls
- Regulatory issues
- Can be diversified away through portfolios

### Hedging Strategies

- **Forward Contracts**: Agreement to buy/sell at future date at fixed price
- **Futures**: Standardized exchange-traded forward contracts
- **Options**: Right (not obligation) to buy or sell at strike price
  - Call option: Right to buy
  - Put option: Right to sell
- **Swaps**: Exchange of cash flows (interest rate, currency)

### Value at Risk (VaR)
Estimates maximum potential loss over a time period at given confidence level.
- Example: 95% VaR = $1M means 95% probability loss won't exceed $1M

### Credit Risk
Risk of default by borrower:
- **Credit Spread**: Additional yield required for credit risk
- **Probability of Default**: Likelihood borrower cannot pay
- **Loss Given Default**: Percentage of exposure lost in default

## Valuation Methods

### Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)
Projects future free cash flows and discounts to present value:

1. **Project FCF**: Estimate 5-10 years of future cash flows
2. **Terminal Value**: Value at end of projection period
   - Perpetuity Method: FCF(terminal year) × (1+g) / (WACC - g)
   - Exit Multiple Method: Final year EBITDA × Exit multiple
3. **Discount**: Apply WACC to bring to present value
4. **Add Back Non-Operating Items**: Cash, investments, value of subsidiaries

**Advantages:** Based on fundamentals
**Disadvantages:** Sensitive to assumptions, difficult to project far out

### Comparable Company Analysis (Comps)
Compares target to similar public companies:

1. Identify comparable companies with similar:
   - Business model
   - Growth rate
   - Profitability
   - Risk profile

2. Calculate valuation multiples:
   - EV/EBITDA, EV/Revenue, P/E, P/B

3. Apply median multiples to target's metrics

**Advantages:** Market-based, easy to understand
**Disadvantages:** Limited universe, may not find true comparables

### Precedent Transactions
Analyzes historical M&A transactions of similar companies:

1. Identify prior acquisitions of comparable targets
2. Calculate multiples paid (premium, implied value)
3. Apply to current target

**Advantages:** Real market transactions
**Disadvantages:** Few historical deals, dated transactions

### Asset-Based Valuation
Values company based on assets:

- **Book Value**: Accounting value from balance sheet
- **Market Value**: What assets would sell for in open market
- **Replacement Cost**: Cost to replace all assets

Useful for asset-heavy businesses but ignores earning power.

## Financial Markets

### Market Efficiency

**Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH):**
- **Weak Form**: Past prices don't predict future prices
- **Semi-Strong Form**: All public information is reflected in prices
- **Strong Form**: All information (public and private) is reflected in prices

Implications for investing: Active management should underperform index in efficient markets.

### Market Structures

**Primary Market**: Issuance of new securities
- IPOs: Initial public offering
- Secondary offerings: Existing companies issue new shares
- Direct placement: Securities sold directly to investors

**Secondary Market**: Trading of existing securities
- Stock exchanges: NYSE, NASDAQ
- Over-the-counter (OTC): Direct transactions between parties
- Provides liquidity to investors

### Market Indicators

- **Market Cap**: Share price × Shares outstanding
  - Large-cap: > $10 billion
  - Mid-cap: $2-10 billion
  - Small-cap: < $2 billion

- **Market Index**: Weighted average of group of securities
  - S&P 500: 500 largest US companies
  - Dow Jones: 30 large-cap US companies
  - Nasdaq: 3,000+ US stocks, tech-heavy
  - Russell 2000: Small-cap US stocks

- **VIX (Volatility Index)**: Implied volatility of S&P 500
  - Higher VIX = Greater market uncertainty
  - Traded as fear gauge

### Interest Rates & Yield Curve

- **Yield Curve**: Plot of bond yields by maturity
  - Normal: Upward sloping (long rates > short rates)
  - Inverted: Downward sloping (often precedes recession)
  - Flat: Long and short rates similar

- **Term Structure of Interest Rates**: Explains shape of yield curve
  - Liquidity Preference: Investors demand premium for longer maturities
  - Expectations Hypothesis: Curve reflects expectations of future rates
  - Segmentation Theory: Maturity preferences of different investor types

### Derivatives Markets

**Options:**
- **Call Option**: Right to buy at strike price
- **Put Option**: Right to sell at strike price
- Valuation: Black-Scholes model considers spot price, strike, time, volatility, rates

**Futures:**
- Standardized contracts traded on exchanges
- Daily mark-to-market
- Used for hedging and speculation

**Swaps:**
- Interest rate swaps: Exchange fixed for floating rates
- Currency swaps: Exchange cash flows in different currencies
- Credit default swaps: Insure against bond default

## Financial Analysis Framework

### DuPont Analysis
Breaks down ROE into components:

**ROE = Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier**

- **Net Profit Margin** (Profitability): Operating efficiency
- **Asset Turnover** (Efficiency): Asset utilization
- **Equity Multiplier** (Leverage): Financial leverage

Helps identify where performance issues originate.

### Common-Size Financial Statements
Express all items as percentage of base (usually revenue or assets):
- Facilitates comparison across time periods
- Enables comparison across different-sized companies
- Highlights structural changes in business

### Trend Analysis
Analyzes financial metrics over multiple periods to identify:
- Growth or decline patterns
- Seasonal variations
- Structural shifts
- Management effectiveness

### Variance Analysis
Compares actual results to budget or forecast:
- Identifies significant deviations
- Investigates root causes
- Helps refine forecasting
