## Constitutional Framework

### The Constitution of India
- **Adopted**: January 26, 1950
- **Framed by**: Constituent Assembly
- **Chairman**: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
- **Type**: Sovereign Democratic Republic
- **Features**: Parliamentary democracy with written constitution

### Preamble to the Constitution
The Constitution begins with the Preamble which states the objectives:
- Sovereign Democratic Republic
- Secure: Justice, Liberty, Equality
- Promote: Fraternity, Dignity of individual, Unity and integrity of nation

### Separation of Powers
- **Executive**: President, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers, State Governors
- **Legislative**: Lok Sabha (Lower House), Rajya Sabha (Upper House), State Assemblies
- **Judicial**: Supreme Court, High Courts, District Courts

## The Indian Legal System

### Sources of Indian Law
1. **Constitution of India**: Supreme law of the land
2. **Legislation**: Acts and statutes passed by Parliament and State Assemblies
3. **Case Law**: Judicial precedents established by courts
4. **Customary Law**: Traditional practices recognized by law
5. **International Treaties**: Treaties and conventions ratified by India

### Rule of Law
- All persons, including government officials, are subject to law
- Established through Article 32 (Supreme Court can issue writs) and Article 226 (High Courts can issue writs)
- Principles established through landmark cases like ADM Jabalpur v. Shiv Kanth Singh

### Judicial Review
- Power of courts to review legislative and executive actions
- Can declare acts/laws unconstitutional if they violate the Constitution
- Established in Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)

## Fundamental Rights and Duties

### Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12-35)

#### Article 14 - Right to Equality
- Equality before law
- Equal protection of law
- No discrimination based on religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth

#### Article 15 - Prohibition of Discrimination
- State shall not discriminate on ground of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth
- Exception: Special provisions for scheduled castes and scheduled tribes

#### Article 16 - Equality of Opportunity in Matters of Public Employment
- Equality of opportunity in public employment
- Reservations for SC/ST and OBC candidates
- Exception: Armed forces and certain positions

#### Article 17 - Abolition of Untouchability
- Abolition of untouchability
- Enforcing untouchability declared as offense

#### Article 18 - Abolition of Titles
- No titles shall be conferred by the State
- Exception: Military and academic distinctions allowed

#### Article 19 - Protection of Certain Rights
Six freedoms:
1. Freedom of speech and expression
2. Freedom to assemble peaceably without arms
3. Freedom to form associations
4. Freedom to move freely throughout India
5. Freedom to reside and settle in any part of India
6. Freedom to practice profession, occupation, trade, or business

**Restrictions on Article 19 freedoms allowed** for:
- Sovereignty and integrity of India
- Security of state
- Public order
- Decency or morality

#### Article 20 - Protection in Respect of Conviction for Offences
1. No ex-post facto law application
2. No double jeopardy
3. No self-incrimination

#### Article 21 - Right to Life and Personal Liberty
- Protection of life and personal liberty
- No person can be deprived except by law
- Interpreted broadly to include: right to health, education, work, dignified living, privacy

#### Article 21A - Right to Free and Compulsory Education
- State shall provide free and compulsory education to children

#### Article 22 - Protection Against Arbitrary Arrest and Detention
- No arbitrary arrest
- Right to know grounds of arrest within specified time
- Right to consult lawyer
- Habeas corpus available

#### Article 23-24 - Prohibition of Traffic in Human Beings and Forced Labour
- Prohibition of traffic in human beings
- Prohibition of begar (forced labour)
- Prohibition of child labour

#### Article 25-28 - Freedom of Religion
- Right to freely profess, practice and propagate religion
- State can regulate right on grounds of public order, morality, health
- Right to freedom regarding religious education

#### Article 29-30 - Rights of Minorities
- Rights of cultural, linguistic minorities
- Right to run educational institutions
- Non-discrimination in educational institutions

### Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Articles 51A)
Citizens shall:
1. Abide by Constitution and respect ideals
2. Cherish and follow noble ideals that inspired freedom movement
3. Uphold sovereignty, unity and integrity of India
4. Defend the nation and render national service
5. Promote harmony and spirit of common brotherhood
6. Value heritage and preserve composite culture
7. Protect and improve natural environment
8. Develop scientific temper and spirit of inquiry
9. Safeguard public property
10. Strive for excellence in individual and collective activity

## Directive Principles of State Policy

### Definition and Purpose
Constitutional directives to guide the State in making laws and policies. Non-justiciable but fundamental in governance.

### Economic and Social Rights
- **Article 39**: State shall direct its policy to secure:
  - Right to work
  - Fair wages and conditions of work
  - Equal pay for equal work
  - Protection of children from exploitation
  - Free legal aid to poor

- **Article 41**: Right to work, education, and public assistance
- **Article 42**: Just and humane conditions of work
- **Article 43**: Living wage and social security
- **Article 43A**: Worker participation in management

### Education and Culture
- **Article 45**: Free and compulsory education for children
- **Article 48**: Protection of cows and improvement of cattle
- **Article 51**: Promotion of international peace and security

### Environmental Protection
- **Article 48A**: Protection of environment and forests
- **Article 51A(g)**: Duty to protect environment (Fundamental Duty)

## Major Indian Acts and Laws

### Criminal Procedure

#### Indian Penal Code (IPC), 1860
- **Scope**: Substantive criminal law for entire India
- **Chapters**: 23 chapters covering various crimes
- **Punishment**: Imprisonment, fine, death penalty (in rarest case)

**Major Sections**:
- **Section 34**: Acts of several persons in furtherance of common intention
- **Section 149**: Unlawful assembly
- **Sections 300-304**: Murder, culpable homicide, causing death by negligence
- **Section 302**: Murder - life imprisonment or death penalty
- **Section 307**: Attempt to murder
- **Section 308**: Attempt to cause death
- **Sections 372-376**: Offences relating to women
- **Section 376**: Rape
- **Sections 379-465**: Theft, criminal breach of trust, forgery
- **Section 420**: Cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery of property

#### Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC), 1973
- **Scope**: Procedural law for criminal justice
- **Amendments**: Updated to 2023
- **Key Features**:
  - Right to bail
  - Investigation process
  - Arrest and detention procedures
  - Trial procedures
  - Punishment and sentencing

**Major Provisions**:
- **Section 41A**: Notice for appearance instead of arrest
- **Section 42-50**: Arrest procedures
- **Section 53-60**: Examination of accused and bodily searches
- **Section 161-162**: Examination of witnesses
- **Section 302**: New Code allows more liberal bail provisions

### Civil Procedure

#### Civil Procedure Code (CPC), 1908
- **Scope**: Procedural law for civil disputes
- **Amendments**: Recent amendments in 2023
- **Key Features**:
  - Filing and admission of suits
  - Pleadings
  - Evidence
  - Judgment and orders
  - Appeals

#### Indian Contract Act, 1872
- **Scope**: Law relating to contracts
- **Key Concepts**:
  - Offer and acceptance (Sections 2-13)
  - Consideration (Section 2(d))
  - Capacity to contract
  - Consent and free agreement
  - Legality of contract
  - Performance of contract

**Important Sections**:
- **Section 23**: Consideration or object unlawful if it violates laws
- **Section 27**: Agreements in restraint of trade void

#### Indian Succession Act, 1925
- **Scope**: Law of succession and inheritance
- **Key Areas**:
  - Hindu Succession (with amendments for gender equality)

## Court System Structure

### The Supreme Court of India
- **Composition**: Chief Justice + 33 Judges (can be increased)
- **Seat**: New Delhi
- **Jurisdiction**:
  - **Original**: Between government of India and states, or between states
  - **Appellate**: From High Courts
  - **Advisory**: On matters referred by President
  - **Writ**: Under Article 32 (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Quo Warranto, Certiorari)

### High Courts
- **Number**: 25 High Courts across India
- **Composition**: Chief Justice + Judges (varies by court)
- **Jurisdiction**:
  - Supreme authority in respective states/union territories
  - Original jurisdiction over constitutional matters
  - Appellate jurisdiction from subordinate courts
  - Writ jurisdiction under Article 226

### District Courts
- **Structure**: Trial courts in districts
- **Judges**: District Judge (civil), Additional Sessions Judge (criminal)
- **Jurisdiction**:
  - Original jurisdiction in civil and criminal matters
  - Appeals from subordinate courts
  - Supervisory authority over lower courts

### Subordinate Courts
- **Civil**: Munsiff Courts, Civil Judge Courts
- **Criminal**: Magistrate Courts (Class I-IV)
- **Jurisdiction**: Lower-value disputes and less serious offenses

### Specialized Courts
- **Economic Offences Courts**: Financial crimes
- **Family Courts**: Family matters
- **Labor Courts**: Industrial disputes
- **Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal (TDSAT)**
- **National Green Tribunal**: Environmental matters
- **National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission**: Consumer protection

## Criminal Law

### Classification of Crimes

#### Crimes Against the State
- **Sedition** (Section 124A IPC): Challenging sovereign authority (controversial provision)
- **Waging War Against India** (Section 121 IPC): Acts with intent to wage war
- **Conspiracy** (Section 120A IPC): Agreement between two or more persons

#### Crimes Against Property
- **Theft** (Section 378 IPC): Dishonest taking and carrying away property
- **Robbery** (Section 390 IPC): Theft with violence or threat
- **Dacoity** (Section 391 IPC): Robbery by 5+ persons
- **Criminal Breach of Trust** (Section 405 IPC)
- **Forgery** (Section 463 IPC): Making false documents

#### Crimes Against the Person
- **Assault** (Section 351 IPC): Attempt or threat to cause injury
- **Criminal Intimidation** (Section 503 IPC): Threatening injury
- **Wrongful Restraint** (Section 339 IPC): Preventing movement
- **Wrongful Confinement** (Section 342 IPC): Illegal imprisonment
- **Hurt** (Section 319 IPC): Act causing bodily pain or disease
- **Grievous Hurt** (Section 320 IPC): Serious hurt
- **Wound** (Section 375 IPC): Break in skin

#### Crimes of Violence
- **Culpable Homicide** (Section 299 IPC): Causing death without intention or knowledge
  - **Simple** (Section 304): Punishment up to 2 years
  - **Not amounting to Murder** (Section 304): Up to 10 years
- **Murder** (Section 302 IPC): Intention to cause death or knowledge that act likely to cause death
  - Punishment: Life imprisonment or death penalty
  - "Rarest of rare cases" doctrine from Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab
- **Attempt to Murder** (Section 307 IPC): Attempt with intention to cause death
- **Rape** (Section 376 IPC, as amended in 2013)
  - Definition expanded
  - Includes marital rape (with exceptions)
  - Punishment: 10 years to life imprisonment

### Defenses

#### Insanity (Section 84)
- Insanity of mind at time of act
- Doesn't know nature/quality of act or that it's wrong
- Burden on accused to prove on balance of probabilities

#### Intoxication (Section 85-86)
- Involuntary intoxication: Defense
- Voluntary intoxication: Not defense for crimes requiring specific intent (except Article 304 rash/negligent act)

#### Duress (Section 94)
- Act under compulsion by threat
- Doesn't apply to murder and certain offenses

#### Necessity (Section 81)
- Act done to avoid present harm or danger
- Reasonable belief in existence of circumstances

## Civil Law

### Tort Law
- Wrongful act (other than breach of contract) causing damage
- Damages awarded to compensate victim

#### Types of Torts
- **Negligence**: Breach of duty of care causing damage
  - Landmark case: Donoghue v. Stevenson applied in India
  - Res Ipsa Loquitur: Speaks for itself
  
- **Defamation**: False statement damaging reputation
  - Slander: Oral false statement
  - Libel: Written false statement
  - Defense: Truth, qualified privilege, fair comment

- **Private Nuisance**: Unreasonable use of property causing damage
  - Interference with enjoyment of land
  - Burden: Balance of convenience test

- **Breach of Duty**: Violation of legal obligation
  - Contractual duty
  - Statutory duty

#### Damages
- **Compensatory**: Restore to original position
- **Nominal**: Small amount when no real loss
- **Exemplary/Punitive**: Punish for gross misconduct
- **Contemptuous**: Minimal damages with disapproval

### Contract Law

#### Essential Elements of Contract
1. Offer and acceptance
2. Consideration (Section 2(d): Something of value)
3. Intention to create legal relations
4. Parties capable of contracting
5. Lawful object and consideration

#### Types of Contracts
- **Valid Contract**: Binding and enforceable
- **Void Contract** (Section 2(g)): No legal effect from inception
- **Voidable Contract** (Section 2(i)): Enforceable at option of one party
- **Illegal Contract**: Prohibited by law, void
- **Contingent Contract** (Section 31): Depends on event

#### Discharge of Contract
- By agreement/mutual consent
- By performance
- By breach
- By supervening impossibility
- By operation of law

### Family Law

#### Hindu Law
- Governed by Hindu Marriage Act, 1955
- Grounds for divorce: Adultery, cruelty, desertion, insanity, conversion, separation (2+ years)
- Maintenance: Section 25 HMA and Section 125 CrPC
- Custody: Best interest of child principle

#### Maintenance Under CrPC
- **Section 125**: Maintenance of wife, children, aged parents
- **Order**: Magistrate can order monthly maintenance
- **Non-compliance**: Imprisonment up to 30 days and fine

#### Guardianship and Custody
- **Guardianship of Infants Act, 1890**: Applies to non-Hindus
- **Custody**: Paramount consideration is welfare of child
- **Guardians**: Parents, court-appointed guardians
- **Tests**: Best interest test, welfare paramountcy doctrine

## Administrative Law

### Constitutional Safeguards

#### Writs (Article 32 - Supreme Court, Article 226 - High Courts)
1. **Habeas Corpus**: Release from unlawful detention
2. **Mandamus**: Court order commanding official duty
3. **Prohibition**: Supreme court restraining inferior court
4. **Quo Warranto**: Challenge to officer's authority
5. **Certiorari**: Quashing of judicial order

#### Judicial Review
- Courts review legislative and executive actions
- Cannot override legislation on merits, only constitutionality
- Grounds: Violation of constitutional rights, violations of law, unreasonableness

### Administrative Procedure

#### Audi Alteram Partem (Hear the other side)
- Right to be heard
- Right to know case against you
- Opportunity to present defense

#### Nemo Judex in Causa Sua (No one is judge in their own case)
- No bias rule
- Apparent bias disqualification
- Established in Liversidge v. Anderson

#### Natural Justice Principles
- Notice and hearing
- Impartial adjudication
- Reasoned decision

### Public Interest Litigation (PIL)
- Citizens can seek judicial review on public interest
- Relaxed locus standi requirements
- Established through: S.P. Gupta v. Union of India, Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. Union of India
- Used for: Environmental protection, public rights, social justice

## Constitutional Amendments

### Amendment Process (Article 368)
- **Simple Majority**: Budget, administrative matters
- **Absolute Majority**: Most amendments
- **Special Majority** (2/3 of present + voting + 1/2 of total): Basic structure amendments
- **Ratification by States**: For federal aspects (1/2 states)

### Major Amendments

#### First Amendment (1951)
- Added restriction to fundamental rights
- Education and agriculture related

#### 14th Amendment (1962)
- Changed "State" definition in Article 12

#### 42nd Amendment (1976)
- Emergency period amendment
- Added Fundamental Duties
- Changed "Socialist Secular Democratic" in Preamble
- Termed as "Mini Constitution"

#### 44th Amendment (1978)
- Restricted presidential emergency powers
- Made property right non-fundamental

#### 86th Amendment (2002)
- Made education a fundamental right (Article 21A)

#### 104th Amendment (2015)
- Goods and Services Tax provision (Article 279A)

#### Basic Structure Doctrine
- From Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)
- Parliament cannot amend basic structure of Constitution
- Features: Sovereignty, democratic character, secularism, individual liberty, federalism, judicial review

## Key Constitutional Principles and Doctrines

### Principle of Equality
- **Formal Equality**: Equal treatment before law
- **Substantive Equality**: Equal access to resources and opportunities
- **Proportionality Test**: Government action must be proportionate to objective

### Principles of Social Justice
- **Distributive Justice**: Fair allocation of resources
- **Redressal Justice**: Remedy for wrongs
- **Positive Discrimination**: Reservations for marginalized groups

### Right to Privacy
- Recognized under Article 21 (Right to Life)
- Landmark: Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017)
- Privacy rights include: Data privacy, bodily autonomy, informational privacy

### Doctrine of Legitimate Expectation
- Administrative decisions should not betray reasonable expectations
- Based on honest representation or past practice
- Established in E.P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu

### Doctrine of Proportionality
- Government action must be proportionate to objective
- Established in: State of Maharashtra v. Mangturam

### Doctrine of Pith and Substance
- Courts examine real substance of legislation
- To determine legislative competence
- Not just title or form

## Legal Procedures Overview

### Criminal Trial Procedure (CrPC, 1973)

#### Investigation Stage
- **Section 156**: FIR (First Information Report) registered
- **Section 161-162**: Witness examination
- **Section 53-60**: Medical examination of accused

#### Pre-trial
- **Sections 41-60**: Arrest and detention procedures
- **Section 67**: Right to bail
- **Supreme Court Guidelines**: D.K. Basu guidelines for arrest

#### Trial Stages
1. **Framing of charge** (Section 225): Charges formally read to accused
2. **Prosecution evidence** (Sections 233-239): Witness examination and cross-examination
3. **Defense case** (Sections 240-243): Accused can present evidence
4. **Arguments**: Both sides present arguments
5. **Judgment**: Acquittal or conviction with reasoning

#### Sentencing
- **Aggravating factors**: Brutality, premeditation, vulnerability of victim
- **Mitigating factors**: First offense, remorse, reformation potential
- **Rigorous vs Simple Imprisonment**: Varying severity levels

### Civil Trial Procedure (CPC, 1908)

#### Filing Suit
- **Plaint**: Written statement of claim
- **Jurisdiction**: Territorial and pecuniary jurisdiction determined
- **Limitation**: Must be filed within limitation period (varies by type)

#### Pleadings
- **Plaint** (Section 26): Plaintiff's statement
- **Written Statement** (Section 63): Defendant's response
- **Replication**: Plaintiff's response to written statement

#### Evidence
- **Oral Evidence**: Witness testimony
- **Documentary Evidence**: Documents, records
- **Expert Evidence**: Scientific, technical opinions
- **Rules of Evidence**: Indian Evidence Act, 1872

#### Judgment and Decrees
- **Judgment** (Section 213): Court's pronouncement on case
- **Decree** (Section 33): Binding order

#### Appeals and Review
- **Appeal** (Section 100): Challenge judgment in higher court
- **Revision** (Section 115): Challenge judgment on law only
- **Review** (Section 114): Review based on new evidence

## Recent Developments

### Criminal Procedure Code, 2023
- Replaced CrPC 1973
- Stricter bail provisions in certain cases
- Technology integration (digital evidence, video recording)
- Victim-centric approach

### Bharatiya Nyay Sanhita (BNS), 2023
- Replaces Indian Penal Code, 1860
- Defines crimes, punishments, procedures
- Maintains substantive criminal law but with amendments
- Effective Date: July 1, 2024

### Bharatiya Sakshya Act (Evidence Act), 2023
- Replaces Indian Evidence Act, 1872
- Updates evidence rules for digital era
- Digital evidence provisions
- Hearsay exceptions clarified

### Bharatiya Stree Adalatnama (BSA) Bill
- Proposed new family law code
- Uniform provisions across religions
- Gender-neutral provisions

## Important Legal Maxims in Indian Law

- **Nulla Poena Sine Lege**: No punishment without law
- **Nullum Crimen Sine Lege**: No crime without law
- **Res Judicata**: Matter decided cannot be tried again
- **Estoppel**: Cannot deny facts previously accepted
- **Caveat Emptor**: Buyer beware in contracts
- **Mens Rea**: Guilty mind (criminal law)
- **Actus Reus**: Guilty act (criminal law)
- **Proxima Causa**: Proximate cause
- **Vis Maior**: Act of God (force majeure)

## Glossary of Terms

- **Affidavit**: Written statement on oath
- **Bail**: Release from custody pending trial
- **Caveat**: Notice to court not to pass order without notice
- **Contempt of Court**: Violation of court's dignity or authority
- **Credibility**: Believability of witness
- **Decree**: Final order of court
- **Default**: Failure to appear or comply
- **Deposition**: Witness testimony under oath
- **Estoppel**: Prevented from denying previous statement
- **Ex Parte**: Without other party present
- **Fiat**: Court order or authorization
- **Habeas Corpus**: Release from unlawful detention
- **Injunction**: Court order to do or not to do something
- **Interim Order**: Temporary court order
- **Jurisdiction**: Authority of court to hear case
- **Locus Standi**: Right to bring action
- **Mandamus**: Court order to perform duty
- **Mens Rea**: Guilty mind, criminal intention
- **Mitigation**: Reducing severity
- **Moot**: No practical purpose anymore
- **Order**: Direction of court
- **Parole**: Conditional release from imprisonment
- **Precedent**: Previous court decision used as authority
- **Probation**: Conditional release without imprisonment
- **Pro Bono**: Legal work without fee
- **Quash**: Cancel or void
- **Res Judicata**: Thing adjudged, matter decided
- **Restitution**: Restoration to original condition
- **Statute of Limitations**: Time limit for bringing action
- **Subpoena**: Order to attend court or produce document
- **Tort**: Civil wrong
- **Writ**: Court order
